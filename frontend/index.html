How to spot a phishing email

  1. Sender address – does the real address match who it claims to be?
  2. Domain spelling – look for swapped characters (0/o, 1/l) and extra words.
  3. Unexpected urgency – pressure to act "now" is a manipulation tactic.
  4. Suspicious links – hover to preview; raw IPs and odd domains are red flags.
  5. Credential requests – real services rarely ask for passwords by email.
  6. Unexpected attachments – especially .exe, .js, .zip or double extensions.
  7. Generic greetings – "Dear customer" can signal a mass mailing.
  8. Unusual payment requests – gift cards, new bank details, secrecy.
  9. Threatening language – account closure, legal action.
  10. Context – were you expecting this message? No single sign proves phishing.

Before you click checklist

  • ☐ Do I know and expect this sender?
  • ☐ Does the domain look exactly right?
  • ☐ Is it pressuring me to hurry?
  • ☐ Can I reach the site by typing the official address myself?
  • ☐ Is it asking for passwords, codes or personal data?
  • ☐ Did I verify through a second, trusted channel?
  • ☐ If unsure, did I report it instead of clicking?

HTTPS (the padlock) only means the connection is encrypted – phishing sites can have it too.
